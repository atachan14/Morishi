# New-run continuous soil model

`reference-stats-2` / `direct-continuous-soil-2` / `element-root-save-5` apply only to new runs. Old finite, attribute-specific runs retain their saved model and coefficients. The UI writes resumed v4 runs to `morishi.reference-root.v1.working`; their original key is preserved. New v5 runs use `morishi.reference-root-continuous.v1`. Normal Reference saves similarly protect the original with `.working` and use `morishi.reference-normal-continuous.v1` for the new model. Legacy v1/v2/v3 originals also remain protected.

Soil is a persistent spatial supply intensity, not an inventory. Its fixed sampled weights do not decrease. HP, Aura, Root, Resist, Power, Tech, Range and Speed each use a 1000-unit spatial normalization; this is a distribution calibration, not finite stock. The five common ElementStats each receive one baseline, never the sum of four former 1000-unit attribute inventories. Local composition divides the area supply among these eight active nutrients. The removed HPReg target is not redistributed via a 20/19 multiplier. The new eight-kind composition is an explicit model change.

Legacy twenty-column vectors remain a serialization carrier. In new soil only, indices 0/1/2/3/4/5/6/7 mean HP/Tech/Aura/Root/Power/Range/Speed/Resist; indices 8–19 are zero. Corpse vectors keep their original attribute-specific meaning. Plant stats index 1 always remains zero in new runs. Soil Tech is separately credited to ReferenceStats.tech, never HPReg.

Root reference radius remains 100u. Soil and corpse area rates remain 2/sec, water uptake 4/sec, and growth water 1 per acquired nutrient. HP/Aura/Root/Power/Range/Speed/Resist gains remain 1/.05/2.5/.2/.1/.002/.001. New Tech uses provisional gain .001, matching the existing Resist gain; this is declared configuration, not an adopted balance reduction. The previously proposed reference radius 300 and area rates 1/.5 are not applied.

Ground and plant water, and corpse nutrients, remain finite. External soil acquisition is recorded separately from finite corpse and water ledgers. Each tick transfers water, pays Skills in their existing fixed order, then spends remaining water once on direct nutrition. Root area, local 1/n competition, overlap, sunlight and contact limits bound continuous supply. Night and enemy contact stop nutrient growth.

Each species defines `elementWeights` with nonnegative weights summing to one. Existing art elements receive their matching single attribute; unclassified/initial species deterministically use fire. These are provisional editable definitions. New runs snapshot the catalog into their save. Soil effective gains and Tech are allocated using the species classified before that tick's acquisition. A 70/30 species receives Power10 as fire7/ice3. Size is finalized, effective gains are rebased once, and effective stats determine the next classification. Existing stats are never moved on metamorphosis. Corpses retain their original attribute allocation. Generated defenses and player common generation limits remain unchanged.

New runs have no HPReg generation, nutrition growth, automatic recovery, Reg overlay or HPBar Reg label. Compatibility-only legacy seed/vector fields remain readable. Skill healing and MaxHP increases adding current HP remain available. Future PlayerPanel/CreateBonus excludes HpReg; MN and PlayerPanel remain unimplemented design tasks.

Validation: renewable repetition beyond the old inventory cap, local competition and order invariance, finite corpse depletion, water/night/contact caps, conserved 70/30 allocation, HPReg absence, legacy recovery, reference immediate/future gain, species change without reallocating history, v5 next-tick replay, and v4 original protection.

The editable defaults are public/plant-catalog.json. After changing them, run the local `scripts/sync-nutrient-species.mjs` generator. A regression test checks the bundled simulation snapshot matches the editable catalog. Soil Tech gain is explicit in GameConfig and copied into each new run's DirectRules, so a save retains its chosen coefficient.
